Consider the numbers

Think in terms of jobs, not just website cost.

A website can support your marketing, but it cannot promise a certain number of leads or sales. A useful starting point is to compare the build cost with the gross profit you typically earn from a new job.

Website cost ÷ gross profit per new job = approximate jobs to cover the build cost.

Use gross profit after direct job costs, not total customer revenue. Your actual results depend on demand, competition, your offer, follow-up, and other factors.

Illustrative example

If a new job contributes $500 in gross profit:

  • Starter at $1,200: about 3 additional jobs to cover the build cost.
  • Growth at $2,500: 5 additional jobs to cover the build cost.

This is a simple break-even illustration, not a forecast or guarantee. Domain, hosting, and other business costs are separate.

Is now the right time?

The best investment is one that fits your business today.

A website is most useful when it solves a real problem and you’re ready to respond to the opportunities it may help create.

A website may be a good next step if…

  • Customers ask for a website before they feel ready to contact you.
  • Your current site is outdated, hard to use on mobile, or unclear about your services.
  • You’re ready to take on suitable new work and want a more professional home base online.

It may make sense to wait if…

  • You’re already at capacity and don’t want additional inquiries.
  • Your services or target customers are still changing significantly.
  • Another business expense would put pressure on cash flow right now.

Common questions about the investment

Will a new website guarantee more customers?

No. A website can help people understand and contact your business, but it can’t guarantee rankings, inquiries, or revenue. Results also depend on demand, your reputation, your offer, and how inquiries are handled.

Do I need a website if I already have a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile helps people find basic business information. A website gives you more room to explain your services, show examples, answer questions, and guide customers to contact you. Whether you need both depends on your business and goals.
